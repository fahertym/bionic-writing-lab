### Correction to the previous session's size claim

The prior artifacts described Gospel as "an 84 KB document" and "~93,500 words

- The 84 KB is Google Docs `fileSize` — formatting and metadata overhead. The
  actual text is **5,807 words**, essay scale.
- The ~93,500 words of October sessions are **word-count metadata only.** The

**Gospel is not a book. It is a short work.**

### Central thesis

Empire is a system that must be dismantled *twice* — once in the world, once
inside the people it formed — and what replaces it is not a better ruler but a
covenant among equals with no ruler at all.

### Register and audience

Prophetic, incantatory, second-person plural. It addresses "the people," not a
reader deciding what to believe. **This is the one register in the corpus that
is not adversarial.** Every other major work argues against a defence; Gospel
addresses those it assumes are already suffering.

### The theological position — the striking part

**There is no God in it. There is no Jesus in it. There is no Christianity in
it.** The "Spirit of Liberation" is a personified abstraction that never
claims metaphysical status, and "Justice" is a human prophet from the
"suffering masses."

`SUPPORTED_INTERPRETATION`: Gospel **takes scripture's form and empties it of
deity.** It keeps the covenant, the prophet, the verse numbering, the woes
("Woe to you who have grown fat upon the blood of the poor") and discards the
sovereign. That is a specific and deliberate operation, and it is the inverse
of what *Villain* does: *Villain* prosecutes the deity and leaves the text
standing as evidence; *Gospel* discards the deity and keeps the literary form
as a vehicle.

### Its claims, by domain

- **Economic:** markets are temples with priests; competition is a device for
  division; cooperation is the alternative; *"what is wealth when the people
  cease to believe in its worth?"*
- **Political:** no kings, no rulers, no masters; councils and consensus;
  *"authority is granted only by the consent of the governed"*
- **Governance:** self-government through councils and assemblies; leadership
  redefined as service; explicit rejection of concentration
- **Empire:** built on sand; maintained through division of worker against
  worker, race against race, nation against nation
- **Ecological:** the earth as trust rather than possession
- **Provision:** *"No person shall go hungry, no person shall be without
  shelter, and no person shall suffer alone"*
- **Liberation theology:** it shares the preferential option for the poor and
  the prophetic register, but **drops the theology.** It is liberation
  theology's form without its God.

## 5. Against the established corpus

### vs *The Radical Carpenter* (2023)
**Everything Christian is gone.** Radical Carpenter argues Jesus was a
socialist and modern Christianity could be realigned with him. Gospel keeps the
scriptural *form*, the prophetic voice and the economic reading of temples, and
**removes Jesus entirely**. The prophet is a human named Justice. This is a
real and datable move: reform-of-Christianity → appropriation-of-its-form.

### vs the American trilogy (2024)
Same targets, opposite mode. Empire, markets, division, manufactured scarcity,
manufactured consent — all present. But the trilogy **diagnoses at length with
evidence**, and Gospel **asserts in one breath without any**. Not a recycling
and not a deepening: a **translation into a different register**, written in
the same season Book 3 stalled.

### vs *The Villain in the Verse* (2025–26)
The interesting comparison.

| God as projected authority | **no** — no God at all |
| moral audit of a deity | **no** |
| punishment critique | **no** |
| moral outsourcing | partial — "no kings, no masters" |
| **preserved function after religion** | **yes, strongly** — covenant, ritual, assembly, shared meaning without a sovereign |
| meaning without submission | **yes** |
| **internalised system** | **yes — Chapter 4, in full** |

### vs ICN
Chapter 3 and Chapter 5 contain, in prophetic register: councils and
assemblies, consensus decision-making, **"authority is granted only by the
consent of the governed"**, "no kings, no rulers, no masters", federation of
tribes retaining difference, commons and stewardship, cooperative production,
and a covenant explicitly framed as **"a living document, open to change and
adaptation."**

Those are conceptual cousins of ICN's scoped authority, consent, subsidiarity
and revisability.

**Explicitly not claimed:** that Gospel influenced ICN. No document connects
them. Conceptual resemblance is not causal evidence, and the same boundary that
governs the Villain adjacency governs this one.

## 6. Does "the critique is in prose, the construction is in code" survive?

**Qualified — the middle outcome the brief anticipated.**

It does **not** fail. Gospel contains no mechanism: no dispute resolution at
scale, no economics, no enforcement, no failure modes, no account of what
happens when a council is captured. Article 3 rejects hierarchy without saying
how a large group decides anything. ICN remains the only place detailed
machinery exists.

It does **not** survive unqualified either. Gospel supplies **constructive
principles and institutional direction in readable prose**, which the prior
session said did not exist. The five covenant articles are a genuine
articulation of what the alternative is *for*.

**Revised formulation:**

> The diagnosis is in prose. The principles are in *Gospel of Liberation*. The
> mechanism is in code. Nothing yet connects the three, and that gap is the
> library's real structural problem.
